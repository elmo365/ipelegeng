Design system and key-screen mockups for a nine-category services marketplace launching in Gaborone and Francistown. Android-first, Flutter, built for low-end phones and 3G.
English only BWP / Pula Android 8+, <30MB Wallet balance, never a payout account
Design reasoning
Five decisions this system is built around
Material 3 bones, Ipelege skin. The app is Flutter/Android-first for a market on low-end hardware — stock Material patterns (bottom nav, list items, filled/outlined buttons) are what these phones and users already know. Novelty spent on the five problems below, not on reinventing navigation.
Thin categories say so, honestly. Comparable marketplaces (Gojek, Grab) win on trust in the first session, not feature count — a home screen that hides low supply looks broken when the customer taps in. Every category tile shows a real, specific count instead of pretending.
Verification and rating are separate signals. Lynk's shutdown traced partly to ratings working too well — new providers with zero reviews never got a first booking. Ipelege surfaces the verified badge (a compliance fact, present from day one) independently of star rating (which only exists after jobs are done).
The app never implies it took your money. Payment happens directly between customer and provider. Every screen near a completed booking says so in plain words, at the moment it matters — not buried in terms.
The wallet balance is a meter, not an account. It is called the wallet balance, plainly, because that is what a provider calls it — but there is no withdraw button, no "available balance" framing and no stored value to reclaim. The disclaimer sits on the balance card itself, not in a footnote.
Journey map
End to end, before any screens
Built from the repo: UC-1 to UC-18 in use-cases.md, the booking, top-up and verification state machines in system-flowcharts.md. Each stage lists the steps a person actually takes, the states the system can be in at that step, and the screens that implies. Screens marked gap are mapped but not yet mocked up. There is no target screen count — the number falls out of the journey, and it is still growing as states get named.
What the app is for
A ride, a moving truck, a room to rent, a plumber and a braider are five separate searches through five informal networks today — four of them with no digital option at all. Ipelege replaces all five with one app, and sells the thing Facebook groups and word of mouth cannot: accountability.
Breadth is the product — categories feed each other Providers pay only when work arrives Customers never pay a platform fee Verification is what's being sold
Two consequences for the journey. Verification isn't a compliance step buried in provider onboarding — it's the value proposition, so it has to be legible at the moment a customer is choosing. And the journey doesn't end at a completed booking: it re-enters discovery in an adjacent category, which is the whole ecosystem thesis and needs its own moment in the UI (stage 7).
One account, two journeys, roles stacked on top
Nobody registers as a provider. Every account is created as a customer and stays one forever; the provider role is applied for later, per category, and never replaces the customer role. A driver still needs a plumber. A landlord still needs a truck. So the app has to carry both journeys at once for the same person, with a way to move between them — and the provider side is not one state but a matrix, because verification is granted, refused and revoked one category at a time.
DAY 1
Customer — always
Granted at registration with no further steps (FR-1.3). Never lost, never switched off, no verification required to book.
LATER, OPT-IN
Provider — per category
Applied for one category at a time (FR-1.4, FR-1.5). Adding a second category is a second application with different documents — an easier conversation, but a full flow.

Customer navigation and provider navigation are different surfaces for the same account. The switch has to exist before the first category is approved, or a pending applicant has nowhere to check on it.

Revocation is the sharpest case: an approved category can be revoked for an expired document or misconduct, and every listing under it deactivates immediately. What happens to bookings already accepted is undefined in the spec — so that state has no honest copy yet.
One balance, every fee, always plus VAT
A provider funds one wallet balance and every Ipelege charge is drawn from it, wherever in the app it originates. There is no pay-as-you-go anywhere: a landlord does not pay a card at the point of listing, a driver does not settle after each ride. They top up, and fees are deducted. And because the fee is Ipelege's own service to the provider, every deduction carries 14% VAT on the fee amount — never on the customer's money, which never passes through the platform at all.
IN
Top up only
Orange Money, EFT or card, into the one balance. Non-redeemable and non-withdrawable — it buys platform fees and nothing else.
OUT
Every fee, one place
Ride commission 8% of the fare · job commission on completion · rental listing fee per room, per vacancy. Each posts as fee + VAT, two lines, never bundled into one opaque figure.
NEVER
Customer money
The fare and the job price are paid directly, person to person, outside the app. The platform is not in that transaction, so it does not VAT it.

The driver keeps the whole fare in hand and pays P10.94 of pre-loaded credit for having been sent the job. Stating it this way round is the point — it is why the balance reads as a meter and not as an account someone is owed money from.
A cancellation does not refund itself
Cancellation only matters to the balance once a fee has already been deducted — a ride called off after dispatch, a completion the customer disputes, a rental listing withdrawn after publishing. In every one of those cases the credit does not come back on its own. A reversal is a separate reviewed event, and until it is confirmed the balance does not move by a thebe.
Fee deducted → Cancellation raised → Under review → Confirmed · reversal posts or Declined · deduction stands
What the wallet does while it waits
The balance is unchanged. The figure at the top of the wallet does not move on a cancellation. Only a confirmed reversal changes it, and the number animates only then.
The ledger carries the pending marker instead. The original deduction rows stay exactly as posted, and a reversal-pending row sits above them with no amount — an amount would imply money already returned.
A reversal mirrors the deduction, line for line. The fee credited back and its VAT credited back, same figures, opposite sign, both referencing the original booking. Never one merged credit.
The VAT reversal needs a credit note. Tax was charged on a fee that is being unwound, so it cannot quietly disappear from the trail — it has to be reversed as its own entry.
Where the mobile side stops
Confirming a cancellation is an adjudication, and adjudication is an admin job on the desktop side, which is out of scope here. The phone shows status and reason — raised, under review, confirmed, declined — and never a decision control.
STILL OPEN
Who may raise a cancellation, and up to when after the fee posts.
Whether some causes reverse automatically — a verified customer no-show, a ride cancelled inside the first minute.
The review window a provider is told to expect, since the copy has to name it.
Partial reversals: a driver who travelled to a no-show has a real claim on part of it, but no rule exists yet.
Declined reversals stay on the ledger with their reason. Whether a provider can contest that is undecided.
The journey has three shapes, not one
The launch categories don't share a flow — two of them skip whole stages. A single linear journey would have been wrong; the stages below apply per shape.
Browse & book · commission
Movers & hauling · Hairdressing & beauty · Plumbing · Electrical · Tiling · Catering · Hire
Stages 0–3 customer side, 4–5 provider side. Commission posts on completion; the service itself is paid outside the app.
Dispatch · commission
Rides
Skips stage 2 entirely — no browse, no listing detail. The request goes to nearby drivers with sufficient credit (FR-3.10), and live tracking is a launch requirement, not an addition.
Pay-per-listing · no booking
Property rentals
No stage 3 at all — no booking, no commission, no completion. The tenant enquires and leaves the app; the landlord pays per room, per vacancy. Verification is the only reason to pay instead of posting free to a group.
Launch sequencing shapes the first run
The opening cluster is rides, movers and rentals, seeded deep rather than wide — movers first, then rentals, then beauty, then the trades, with driver recruitment running in parallel. Public launch waits on a supply threshold per category per city (M5). So at first open, two or three categories will look healthy and three will look thin, for months. That is the design condition, not an edge case — which is why every category tile states a real count instead of hiding it.

STAGE 1
Account
UC-1 · FR-1.1, 1.2, 1.10

Returning users are a separate path: phone → OTP, no consent step unless the consent version was superseded, in which case the consent gate forces re-consent before anything else proceeds.
Session rule: OTP on every fresh login, then stay signed in until explicit logout
Every login is two factors — the number, then the code. Once through, the session persists indefinitely; nothing expires the user out of it except tapping log out. Re-entry to a persistent session is unlocked by device biometric (fingerprint or face), with a device passcode as the fallback where biometry is absent, broken or refused — which on the low-end Android this ships to is common enough that passcode is a first-class path, not a footnote. Biometry unlocks; it never authenticates on its own, so a new device or a fresh install always goes back through phone + OTP.
States: code sent · wrong code · resend cooling down · locked after N attempts · number already registered → sign in · consent declined → cannot proceed · returning to a live session → biometric prompt · biometry unavailable or refused → passcode · new device or reinstall → full phone + OTP again · signed out explicitly → back to sign in

STAGE 4B
Living in both roles
FR-1.6 · user-model.md
Customer view ⇄ Switch mode ⇄ Provider view → My categories → Add another category
The switch is exactly two buttons — Consumer and Service provider. Categories are not in the switch; they live inside provider mode, where all of them sit together on one surface. Switching is a change of mode, not a change of category, and it never asks which service you mean.
The two journeys share one login, so the switch is navigation, not authentication — and it has to work in four different conditions, each of which needs different copy in the same control: never applied, application pending, approved in one or more categories, or approved but currently blocked because the wallet balance ran out. Bookings a provider makes as a customer sit in their customer inbox, not their provider inbox; the two must never mix.

STAGE 7
The loop
A-5 B3 · categories.md
Books a room → "Moving in? Find a truck" → Movers booking → Plumber, water delivery → Back to stage 2, new category
This is the part the journey can't end without. One acquired user is supposed to produce several transactions across several categories — that's the entire argument for launching with six. The spec even names the anchor point: a tenant who takes a room is a mover customer that same week, so the prompt belongs at the rental enquiry, and again at a completed movers job (funeral → catering, chairs, transport; new tenant → plumber, water delivery).
Which category feeds which — the pairs the prompt is built from
Room taken→truck that same week→plumber, water delivery once moved in
Funeral or wedding→catering·chairs, tents and sound hire·transport for guests
Rides→the connective tissue: it physically moves customers to every other provider, and carries the daily frequency that builds the habit
Read the other direction, this is also the seeding argument: each category makes the next one cheaper to fill, because one acquired user produces several transactions across several categories. Which is why the prompt is a real feature and not a cross-sell banner — it is where the breadth thesis either pays off or doesn't.

Does the map hold up?
Mostly, but it exposes three things the 14-screen list in the brief doesn't cover, and two it can't cover yet.
1. There was no way back in. The brief lists onboarding as "phone + OTP" — which registers a user but never signs an existing one back in, and never captures the name UC-1 asks for. Register and sign in are separate screens with different consent behaviour.
2. Visitors browse before they register. UC-4 grants Visitor browse and search, so the account wall sits at the booking action. That needs an auth gate, and it changes the home screen's job: it sells supply to a stranger, not to a logged-in customer.
3. The provider side is a surface, not a screen. Create listing exists; "my listings" doesn't. Neither does the mode switch between customer and provider, the my-categories matrix behind it, a provider home, or the repeat path for adding a second category later. Same for the Facebook opt-in (UC-15, consent-bearing, defaulted off) and account/privacy carrying FR-1.8, FR-1.9 and UC-16 — all Musts.
4. Two states can't be designed yet. NO_SHOW has no fee rule and cancellation handling is unspecified, so those booking states have no honest copy to write. Same for disputes (UC-13, explicitly undesigned).
5. Counting screens by feature undercounts badly. Once every state that needs its own layout is named — eleven booking states, five verification states per category, six mode-switch conditions, five top-up states — the inventory is somewhere near thirty and still opening up as the mapping continues. The screens mocked up so far are the happy path through it.
Three corrections the specs force on screens already built
Payment happens before "mark complete", not after. A-2 orders it: service delivered → customer pays the provider directly → provider marks complete → customer confirms → commission posts. The pay-directly moment currently sits on the completed state; it belongs one step earlier, while the two people are still together.
Listings need a structured catalogue, not a free-text title. Research in the repo is explicit — structured service catalogue per category, and quote-based flows were a major failure point at Lynk. So create-listing should default to fixed or from-pricing with instant booking, and treat "quote on request" as the exception rather than an equal third option.
New providers need a real boost, not just honest copy. The repo's recommendation on ratings is to build them with an explicit new-provider boost, otherwise you manufacture provider churn. Labelling a provider "new" is only half the fix — placement in browse has to give them a first job.
Proposed build order: close the account gap first (splash, register, sign in, OTP as second factor, biometric unlock with passcode fallback, granular consent incl. WhatsApp/SMS channel, auth gate, location permission), then the booking-status state set with the payment moment moved, then the loop prompt in stage 7, then the provider management surface (my listings, verification status, EFT reference/pending, Facebook opt-in defaulted off, takedown-in-progress state), then account & privacy. Disputes, no-shows and cancellations stay unbuilt until those product decisions land — they have no honest copy until then.

Navigation
Getting there, and getting back out
The screens above only show forward motion, which is the smaller half of the problem. Most of what a person does in an app is retrace: leave a listing and return to the same place in the same list, abandon a form and find the fields still filled, back out of a flow that already took money. On Android that all runs through one hardware gesture the app does not own, so its behaviour has to be specified for every context — not left to whatever the widget tree happens to do.
Two tab sets, one login
Each mode has its own bottom bar, and each tab keeps its own stack. Switching mode replaces the bar rather than adding to it, so a provider deep in a listing does not carry that history into the consumer side.

Four tabs each, and the wallet is not one of them — it hangs off the dashboard, because a provider goes there to fix a problem, not to browse. Tabs carry navigation only; every action stays in the context that produced it.
Sideways is not forward
Three movements look similar on screen and must not be built the same way. Getting this wrong is what makes an app feel like it has trapped you.
Push — a listing, a booking, a form step. Adds to the stack, back pops it. The only movement that deepens history.
Lateral — changing tab, changing category, changing the browse filter. Replaces content in place, adds nothing to the stack. Back returns to the home tab, not through every tab visited.
Replace — booking created, listing published, mode switched, OTP verified. The flow behind it is discarded, so back cannot re-enter it and post a second deduction.
The app bar back arrow and the system gesture always do the same thing. Two back affordances with different behaviour is the fastest way to lose someone's trust in a screen.

Code rules
These come from the market, not from taste. Android 8 and up, a binary under 30MB, 3G, handsets with 1 to 2GB of RAM. A dropped frame costs more here than a missing flourish.
✓
DO
Reach for implicit animations first — AnimatedContainer, AnimatedOpacity, AnimatedSwitcher, TweenAnimationBuilder. A controller is warranted only to sequence, reverse or interrupt.
Route every duration through one helper that checks MediaQuery.disableAnimations. Reduce-motion is a battery decision here, not an accessibility edge case.
Animate transform and opacity — SlideTransition, FadeTransition. Width, padding and margin re-run layout on every frame.
Keep the animated subtree small and hold const children outside the builder, so a rebuild costs close to nothing.
Sequence where order carries meaning: reversal rows land, then the balance moves. That is the order the events actually happened in.
Dispose every controller, and test on a real entry-level handset rather than the emulator.
✕
DON'T
No loops. No shimmer sweeps, pulsing dots or spinning marks — they hold the GPU awake and drain a battery that is the user's lifeline.
No celebration on money events. Payment happens person to person outside the app; confetti over a completion we never processed misstates what happened.
Never animate the balance on first paint, and never animate a gate turning red. Errors appear instantly, at motion.none.
No entrance animation on cold start. Content first; motion belongs to change, and nothing has changed yet.
No BackdropFilter, no parallax, no shadow tweens. Cheap on a flagship, visibly dropped frames on 1GB of RAM.
Never gate a tap on an animation finishing. Take the input immediately and let the motion catch up.
