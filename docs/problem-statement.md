# Problem statement

Most service work in Botswana happens informally. Hairdressers, drivers,
tent-and-chair hire operators, home cooks, plumbers, electricians and truck
owners run real businesses, but without fixed premises, a shopfront, or any
reliable channel to reach customers beyond word of mouth and WhatsApp status
posts.

## For providers

Demand is unpredictable and invisible. There is no way to be discovered by
someone outside your existing network, no record of past work to build trust
with a new customer, and no low-risk way to pay for reach — existing advertising
options assume you can afford to spend before you have earned anything.

## For customers

Finding a trusted provider means asking around. There is no single place to
search, compare, see who is available, or hold anyone accountable if the job
goes wrong. The problem repeats from scratch for every category: a ride, a
catering order, a moving truck and a bus ticket are four separate searches
through four separate informal networks.

Property rentals are the sharpest example. In Gaborone the market runs almost
entirely through Facebook groups — no search, no filters, no verification, the
same listing reposted dozens of times, and scams that nobody is accountable for.

## Market gap

The platforms operating in Botswana today are single-category, and most are
foreign-built.

Ride-hailing solves rides. Bus ticketing exists — Motso Tickets, plus regional
aggregators such as Busbud, Intercape and Checkmybus sell Botswana routes — but
adoption appears negligible; these apps have little visible presence among
ordinary users, and booking a seat still typically means calling the operator or
paying at the rank.

No platform connects the full range of everyday services one household actually
uses. A ride, a catering order, a hauling truck, a plumber and a bus seat are
five separate searches through five separate informal networks — and four of
those have no digital option at all.

## Notes on evidence

Two claims here are asserted rather than evidenced, and should be firmed up
before this document goes to anyone outside the team:

- **Incumbent adoption.** "Negligible visibility and adoption" rests on the
  absence of published download figures and on founder observation as a Botswana
  resident. Defensible, but not measured.
- **Customer definition.** This document does not yet define the target
  customer — urban, smartphone-owning, Gaborone-first is the working assumption.
  See [open questions](open-questions.md).
