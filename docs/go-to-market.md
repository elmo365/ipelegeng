# Go-to-market

## Cities

Launch in **Gaborone and Francistown** — the two largest cities in Botswana.

Density is the deciding factor. Marketplaces fail when supply is spread thin;
ride platforms in particular only work above a threshold of drivers per area,
which is why inDrive and similar operate best in the big cities. A thin national
spread would kill the platform before it started.

Smaller towns are a later decision, made with data rather than in advance.

## Opening cluster

**Rides · Movers & hauling · Property rentals.**

Rationale:

- Rides carry independent, daily-frequency demand — they build habit.
- Movers and rentals feed each other directly.
- Rentals and movers currently live in the same broken Facebook-group world, so
  the providers are findable in one place. Provider acquisition for both can run
  as a single effort.

Hairdressers and the remaining categories follow.

## The cold-start problem

This is the hardest part of the project — harder than the build.

The founder position is that provider recruitment is easier once a working app
exists to demonstrate, since you need something concrete to negotiate with. That
is reasonable.

**But the risk must be named in planning, not deferred entirely.** Marketplaces
do not fail on code; they fail on cold start. A customer opens the app, finds
three hairdressers and no trucks, and never returns. First impressions are
spent once.

Practical consequence: seed **deep, not wide**. Twenty genuinely active
providers in one vertical in one city beats three providers in eight verticals.
The ecosystem thesis still holds — it is built cluster by cluster, not
abandoned.

Which city and which category to seed first is a **build decision**, not purely
a marketing one, because it determines what has to be finished first.

## Positioning against incumbents

Ipelege is locally owned and settles in pula. That is a genuine
differentiator against foreign-built ride platforms.

Note the interface is English only (NFR-9) — localisation is not part of the
positioning. Nothing prevents Setswana in support conversations, Facebook copy
or provider recruitment, where it is a human choice rather than a product
feature.

**It is not sufficient on its own.** "We are local" rarely beats inDrive on
price and driver supply. Local ownership works as a supporting story alongside
breadth — not as the primary claim.
